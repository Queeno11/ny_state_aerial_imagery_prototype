We distinguish the latent characteristic of interest from its observed measurement: \(R_i=f(H_i)+\varepsilon_i\) and \(W_i=\Psi_{c_it_i}(R_i)\), where \(i\) indexes tracts, \(c\) cities and \(t\) dates. Observed inputs are a fixed orthogonal mixture \(X_i=(H_i,A_i,Z_{c_i})M\) of signal characteristics, acquisition nuisance and city cues. The baseline errors are independent centered Gumbels, whose differences are logistic. The principal latent functions are the linear \(g_L(H)=u_1+0.6u_2-0.4u_3\) and quadratic \(g_Q(H)=u_1+0.7u_2u_3+0.4(u_3^2-1)\), with \(u=H\mathcal R\) a fixed rotation; each is centered and normalized to unit population within-city difference dispersion. The purpose is to recover a common signal despite heterogeneous measurement and nuisance variation. Evaluation uses independent tracts in both seen and unseen cities. The main distinction throughout is between recovering an ordering, recovering the direction of a tract's change, and recovering its numerical magnitude.

The main estimator minimizes \(\widehat Q(s)=\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal P:Y_{ij}\ne0}w_{ij}\log\{1+\exp[-Y_{ij}(s(X_j)-s(X_i))]\}+\lambda_T\widehat S_T(s)/\widehat V(s)+\lambda_C\widehat S_C(s)/\widehat V(s)+\alpha J(s)\), where \(Y_{ij}=\operatorname{sign}(W_j-W_i)\) and comparisons are restricted to the same city-date cell. For all ordered fitting comparisons, \(w_{ij}=1/[N(n_{ct}-1)]\), so source tracts receive equal total weight. Here \(\widehat V(s)=\tfrac12\sum_{\mathcal P}w_{ij}[s(X_j)-s(X_i)]^2\), and \(\widehat S_k(s)=|E_k|^{-1}\sum_{(a,b)\in E_k}[s(X_a)-s(X_b)]^2\) for temporal or cross-city stable-view links. The ranking term uses raw score differences; dividing the stability terms by dispersion makes those penalties invariant to uniform score rescaling. In the main suite, the score is linear or \(s_\theta(x)=W_3\operatorname{ReLU}\{W_2\operatorname{ReLU}(W_1x+b_1)+b_2\}\), with effective weights and biases parameterized as \(B\tanh(\theta/B)\), \(B=10\). Inputs are standardized using fitting tracts only. The complexity term \(J(s)\) is the squared path norm, reducing to a squared coefficient norm in the linear case. Thus the first term learns observed orderings, stability discourages responses to irrelevant view differences, and optional complexity regularization controls the flexibility of the fitted function.

Training and evaluation were kept separate, but the stopping procedures differed across completed runs. The initial change experiment used fixed Adam and L-BFGS budgets and selected restarts by training objective; its validation holdout did not select intermediate checkpoints. Follow-up single-sample checks instead held out tracts with a validation target of one fifth, selected checkpoints and restarts by observed within-cell validation logistic loss, used patience to stop training, and restored the best validation checkpoint. No latent test signal or true change entered selection. Independent objective and gradient checks, including the derivative of \(\widehat S/\widehat V\), passed. We also appended a correctly specified ML benchmark, \(\hat\beta_{ML}=\arg\min_\beta-\sum_{i\in\mathrm{fit}}\log p_{\varepsilon_i}[\Psi^{-1}_{c_it_i}(W_i)-b_\star(H_i)'\beta]\), omitting a Jacobian term constant in \(\beta\). It estimates coefficients but knows the structural basis, input map, measurement map and error distribution, so it is an explicitly privileged benchmark. Its strong recovery establishes what is attainable with that information; the gap to the ranking estimator cannot be attributed to optimization alone.

**Measurement transformations (E1).** We compared the unlinked ranking estimator with pooled levels, \((\hat\gamma,\hat\beta)=\arg\min\sum_i(\widetilde W_i-\gamma-\beta'X_i)^2\), and log outcomes with cell fixed effects, \((\hat a,\hat\beta)=\arg\min\sum_i(\widetilde{\log W}_i-a_{c_it_i}-\beta'X_i)^2\), where tildes denote global fitting-sample standardization. The focused replicated design used linear latent truth and linear scorers, varying both measurement maps and the distribution of characteristics across cities. Its maps included \(W=b_{ct}e^{0.6R}\), for which log plus cell effects is correctly specified, and \(W=a_{ct}+b_{ct}\exp\{0.6[R+\kappa_{ct}\tanh(R)]\}\), for which that representation generally fails. A separate additive-baseline check used \(W=a_{ct}+b_{ct}e^{0.6R}\). These comparisons isolate robustness to the measurement equation: increasing maps preserve the within-cell labels used by ranking, even when they change observed levels substantially. Ranking retained useful ordering accuracy across maps; correctly specified log plus fixed effects was competitive, while pooled levels generally performed worse. Importantly, misspecified fixed effects could still rank well, including in the focused exponential check. The supported advantage is flexibility beyond the estimated standard FE specifications, rather than universal dominance over FE.

**Noise restrictions and calibration (E2 and a separate city-calibration experiment).** The noise-design checks replaced the baseline errors with variance-standardized heavy tails, mixtures, and scales varying by cell or features. Alongside common logit, the suite estimated heterogeneous logit with pair loss \(\log\{1+\exp[-Y_{ij}\rho_{ct}\Delta s_{ij}/\sqrt{\widehat V(s)}]\}\), \(\rho_{ct}>0\), and an ordinal surrogate \(\Lambda[-Y_{ij}\Delta s_{ij}/(\tau\sqrt{\widehat V(s)})]\), where \(\Lambda\) is the logistic function. These broader checks were mainly implementation checks, not a completed substantive Monte Carlo robustness study. A separate calibration simulation estimated \(\Pr(W_j>W_i\mid X,c,t)=\Lambda[\rho_c(\bar s(X_j)-\bar s(X_i))]\), with \(\bar s\) normalized on a common reference sample, comparing one learned common calibration, learned city calibrations, and known true calibrations while fitting the same NN. Allowing city calibrations helped when reliability actually differed across cities, but estimating unnecessary heterogeneity could hurt when reliability was common. This separates flexibility in the measurement map from assumptions about comparison noise: robustness to increasing transformations does not establish robustness to every noise process, and better probability calibration does not itself establish accurate change magnitudes.

**Stable-view information (E3).** We estimated the main criterion with \(\lambda_C=0\), comparing \(\lambda_T=0\) with a positive temporal penalty, while varying the number of labeled cities and using both linear and quadratic latent functions. The replicated flexible-estimation battery used a ReLU scorer and selected \(\alpha\) by observed validation misranking; a complementary battery used a linear scorer. A temporal auxiliary view preserves \(H_i\) and city cues while switching the date and redrawing acquisition features, so it should receive the same latent score without providing another outcome label. This tests whether stable views help the scorer ignore acquisition variation. Temporal links improved latent pair ordering for both linear and flexible estimation, and additional labeled cities improved recovery. The quadratic designs showed the value of allowing interactions and curvature rather than requiring a linear latent score. Later validation-stopped checks also combined temporal and cross-city links, with cross-city views changing city cues while preserving \(H_i\). Their strong performance is promising, but the combined arm also supplied more links and a larger total stability coefficient; a matched-budget comparison is needed to isolate the contribution of cross-city information itself.

**Imperfect stability links (E4).** To isolate link quality, the focused battery held the labeled samples and test panels fixed, used linear truth and a linear scorer, and changed only the auxiliary matches. Exact links used \(\widetilde H_i=H_i\); drift used \(\widetilde H_i=\operatorname{clip}(H_i+\delta v_i)\) with random unit direction \(v_i\); false links replaced selected endpoints with independent draws or moved them systematically toward higher latent values. We estimated the unlinked criterion, the criterion with a fixed temporal weight, and a version selecting \(\lambda_T\) from a validation grid including zero. Mild drift preserved much of the value of the links, while false links weakened their benefits. Validation tuning did not uniformly improve upon the fixed-weight specification. The intuition is that an incorrect stability restriction can force genuine differences to look like nuisance variation, potentially suppressing real changes. Link construction and penalty strength are therefore substantive assumptions of the estimator, and access to a validation set does not automatically resolve bad matching.

**Changes within the same tract (E5 and its follow-up diagnostics).** We generated independent matched before-and-after panels containing stable, improving and deteriorating tracts, varying change size and the fraction of changing endpoints incorrectly offered as stable links. For ranking scores we evaluated \(\widehat\Delta_i=[s(X_{i1})-s(X_{i0})]/\sqrt{\widehat V_{\mathrm{fit}}(s)}\) against \(\Delta_i=f(H_{i1})-f(H_{i0})\); the ML benchmark used its identified latent units directly. We separately measured sign agreement, magnitude error and detection, declaring a change when \(|\widehat\Delta_i|\) exceeded the 95th percentile of absolute predicted changes on an independent stable panel. The original fixed-budget NN results showed poor recovery and failed optimization audits, but validation-stopped single-sample checks substantially improved both pair ordering and detection under linear truth, the additive exponential map, and quadratic truth. Direction was often accurately recovered even when temporal-only detection remained weak: residual predicted changes among stable tracts raised the cutoff and caused genuine changes with the correct sign to be missed. Average scale was sometimes reasonable, while individual magnitudes remained noisy or attenuated; conversely, a misspecified linear control could detect changes while understating their amount. The supported limitation is therefore that flexible ordinal recovery has not yet established reliable cardinal measurement of individual changes—not that directions are generally wrong or magnitudes are fundamentally unrecoverable. These scores also do not directly measure changes in monetary units. The validation-stopped nonlinear checks were single replications, so the original full NN experiment still needs re-evaluation under the improved selection protocol.
